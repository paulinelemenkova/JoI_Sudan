\section{Introduction}

Desertification is one of the most destructive climate-related issues in Sudan, especially for its southern and central regions. Desertification is mostly caused by the climatic variations, such as annual decrease of rainfalls with related deficit in moisture \cite{DAWELBAIT201245}. Located southwards of Sahara Desert, between the latitudes 8° and 23°N (see Figure \ref{fig01}), Sudan experiences the especially great risk of desertification. Within the area of the country, the most endangered region prone to desertification lies between 12° to 18°N, including the area surrounding the capital of Sudan -- Khartoum. Climate factors contributing to the hazard of desertification in Sudan include the seasonality of the rainfall patterns \cite{MOHAMED20223265}, the instability, irregularity, and variability of precipitation and the overall water deficit \cite{akhtar1994methods}. This results in high evapotranspiration: 1,900 mm in the extreme north near Sahara and over 2,500 mm in southern Sahel and savannah \cite{ElKarouri1986}. Moreover, flat topography of the country exaggerates the desertification problems of Sudan and increases the effects from dust and sand storms often in the northern regions of the country \cite{9635812,7948717}. Specifically, this includes the effects from the sandstorms and creeping sands and dunes from the areas of Sahara, Nubian and Libyan deserts.

On the other hand, agriculture and lifestock raising play an important role in the economy of Sudan as the major sources of livelihood for inhabitants. However, unsustainable agricultural expansion and farming activities also contribute to the increase of desertification. For instance, soil erosion, exceed in irrigation, compaction and nutrient depletion, increase in pesticides \cite{Nesser}, as well as overgrazing of cattle are some of the results of the uncontrolled agriculture human activities that have serious effects on land structure and significantly contribute to soil degradation and desertification in Sudan.

The process of desertification leads to many negative environmental consequences such as land degradation in arid and semi-arid regions of Sudan. For instance, deep ploughing of the surface increased the susceptibility of soil to wind erosion, which has led to a severe decline in fertility and contributed to the formation of sand dunes \cite{Abdi}. More examples of the effects of the desertification include reduced biological productivity and decrease in plant biomass, increase of the extremely dry desert areas. This naturally results in  deforestation, soil desiccation, changed or affected land cover types and decreased biodiversity. The latter includes, for instance, the extinction of wild species in areas of oases where they dominated. 

The specifics of the Khartoum regions is deeply connected to the Nile River system which is a central water artery of the country of which 70\% belongs to the Nile River Basin \cite{Melesse}. The Nile River serves as a central source of life in Sudan providing water resources for about 85\% of Sudanese population and irrigated agriculture for mixed cultivation of beans, sorghum, and sugarcane \cite{Kauetal2023}. Additionally, it a transport waterway connecting the country with neighbouring territories. Five from the total six Cataracts of Nile are located within Sudan (see Figure \ref{fig01}), while only one (the First Cataract) is situated in Egypt near the Aswan High Dam. The confluence of the Blue Nile and White Nile in Khartoum (Figure \ref{fig02})) results in the high concentration of population in this district which, as a consequence, leads to the increase pressure on the environmental and human pressure in the region. The other important tributaries are the Atbara River (sometimes referred as Red Nile) with high mining activities related to gold and copper mining and cement production \cite{SASMAZ2020106539}.

The importance of Nile River Basin can be illustrated by the fact that roughly 2/3 of its territory lies within Sudan and that its major tributaries -- the While Nile and Blue Nile -- confluence near Khartoum, the capital of Sudan \cite{Hamad}.
